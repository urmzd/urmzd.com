# AI Isn't Taking Your Job. People Are.

The job-loss narrative is a convenient cover for decisions made by people — and the data proves it.

---

Productivity has always been increased by offloading work and creating separate classes of labor. That single principle underpins all of modern society. So why do you keep hearing that you'll lose your job?

### The framing is wrong

People are being laid off because of **people**. A person was involved at some point. But the narrative phrases it as though AI itself will replace the need for you.

What they're really saying is that a system built on statistical pattern matching — a universal function approximator — will replace you. LLMs are a genuine feat of engineering. But they cannot produce information outside their training distribution. They cannot experience emotion. A machine has no sense of consequence.

### Accountability has no substitute

What happens when the system fails? Who is responsible?

Emotions and consequence are evolutionary characteristics trained over thousands of years. The desire to never experience a particular failure again is a powerful motivator for creativity, innovation, and error prevention. You can optimize for a loss function, but you can't cover all possible paths.

### More output means more oversight

Even if AI output were guaranteed to be correct, you'd still need people to define **what correct means**. More output means more accountability, not less.

### The numbers

Between 1979 and 2025, U.S. productivity grew by **92.4%**. Hourly pay grew by just **33.6%**. Productivity has grown **2.7x** as much as pay.

https://www.epi.org/productivity-pay-gap/

This isn't new. The same intentional policy decisions — deregulation, erosion of the minimum wage, corporate globalization, and the hollowing out of union power — have been suppressing wages for over four decades.

**AI is not taking your job. It's being used as an excuse to increase profits.**

### Who's actually hiring?

OpenAI is planning to nearly double its headcount to 8,000. Anthropic is aggressively expanding. NVIDIA continues to grow rapidly.

The companies closest to frontier AI research have not claimed humans are no longer needed. They're hiring **more** people, not fewer.

### You can't eliminate accountability without consequence

When Boeing prioritized cost-cutting over engineering rigor, two 737 MAX aircraft crashed within five months, killing 346 people. Internal pressure led to shortcuts in safety analysis and a culture where profit displaced engineering judgment.

Task automation is real. But the need for strong judgment, oversight, and accountability will always remain.

**That's not a limitation of AI. It's a feature of reality.**
