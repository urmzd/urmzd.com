# AI Isn't Taking Your Job. People Are.

The job-loss narrative is a convenient cover for decisions made by people. The data tells a different story than the headlines.

---

Productivity describes the ratio of output to input. Historically, it has been increased by offloading work and creating separate classes of labor — enabling individuals to claim their time more effectively and focus on higher-impact work. That single principle underpins all of modern society.

So why do you keep hearing that you'll lose yours?

## The Framing Is Wrong

People are being laid off because of *people*. Regardless of whether those decisions were delegated to an AI agent, a person was involved at some point. But the AI job-loss narrative phrases it as though *AI itself* will replace the need for you.

When people talk about "AI," they're almost always talking about Large Language Models — systems that predict the next token in a sequence based on patterns learned from massive training datasets. What they're effectively telling you is that a universal function approximator will replace you. LLMs are a genuine feat of engineering. But they are nowhere near as sophisticated as the human mind. They cannot produce information outside their training distribution. And machines cannot experience emotion. A machine has no sense of consequence.

## Accountability Has No Substitute

What happens when the system fails? Who is responsible? How does a machine understand accountability?

Emotions and consequence are evolutionary characteristics trained over thousands of years — they can't be replaced even with rapid technological advancement. You can optimize for a loss function, but you can't cover all possible paths. The consequences of real decisions — and the desire to never experience a particular failure again — are a powerful motivator for creativity, innovation, and error prevention.

As David Thomas and Andrew Hunt describe it in *The Pragmatic Programmer*: good programmers are paranoid programmers. The same principle extends to any professional.

## More Output Means More Oversight, Not Less

Even if you grant that AI output is guaranteed to be correct — a generous assumption — you'd still need people to define *what correct means*. More output means more accountability, not less.

When was the last time humanity let things run unsupervised without it ending in disaster? Humans don't even trust other humans. Our entire legal infrastructure is structured around that premise.

## Efficiency Creates Demand

Jevons' Paradox: technological improvements that increase efficiency tend to lead to *increased* total consumption — not decreased. As efficiency lowers the effective cost, demand rises to more than offset the savings.

AI enables work and workers to be fluid. Companies no longer require thousands of employees to execute. They can be smaller, distributed into autonomous clusters. Every team becomes autonomous with deep focus on their specific product. That's what you'd expect when productivity is genuinely increasing.

## The Numbers Don't Lie

Between 1979 and 2025, U.S. productivity grew by **92.4%**. Hourly pay grew by just **33.6%**. Productivity has grown **2.7x** as much as pay ([EPI Productivity-Pay Tracker](https://www.epi.org/productivity-pay-gap/)).

This isn't a new trend driven by AI. The same intentional policy decisions — deregulation, erosion of the minimum wage, corporate globalization, and the hollowing out of union power — have been suppressing wages for over four decades. More than 80% of the gap has been driven by rising inequality: compensation flowing disproportionately to the top 1% and 0.1%.

AI is not taking your job. It's being used as an *excuse* to increase profits. The same jobs that are "lost" are often made available again in lower-cost labor markets.

## Why No One Wants to Admit It

**Investor protection.** Investors watching their moats evaporate can reduce the most expensive line item on the balance sheet — human capital — by claiming AI is taking your job.

**Executive disconnect.** Most executives have created so many layers of abstraction between themselves and the actual work that they can no longer reason clearly through the decisions they're making. Abstraction of human thought is collapsing into a broad, thin layer. Everyone needs a sufficient balance of knowledge now — almost the opposite of what we've trended toward in the last 50 years.

**Pressure from capital.** Notice who's actually hiring: OpenAI is planning to nearly double its headcount to 8,000. Anthropic is aggressively expanding. NVIDIA continues to grow. The companies closest to frontier research are hiring *more* people, not fewer.

## You Can't Eliminate Accountability Without Consequence

When Boeing prioritized cost-cutting over engineering rigor, two 737 MAX aircraft crashed within five months, killing 346 people. Internal pressure led to shortcuts in safety analysis and a culture where profit displaced engineering judgment. The company pled guilty to criminal fraud — years after those people could have been saved by adequate oversight.

Task automation is real and does reduce the tedium of certain work. But the need for strong judgment, oversight, and accountability will always remain.

That's not a limitation of AI. It's a feature of reality.
